About the role

As a Brand Strategy Lead at Anthropic, you will own brand architecture and naming strategy across the Anthropic and Claude brands. Claude is becoming part of how people work, build, and discover, and how we organize and name what we make shapes how a broad, global audience of enterprise buyers, developers, researchers, policymakers, and the general public understands it and whether they trust it. This is a foundational role: you'll solidify this function at Anthropic, making the routine calls yourself and bringing the biggest decisions to senior leaders with a clear recommendation.

In this role, you'll own all brand architecture and naming strategy work globally, from how the portfolio fits together as it grows to the names we ship, and you'll build the systems, structure, and processes other teams follow. In a single week, you might map part of the portfolio, generate names, and take multiple recommendations to senior leaders. This is a senior individual contributor role, so you'll scope the work yourself and set a roadmap a year out with minimal direction. You'll partner closely with Brand Design, Product and Brand Marketing, Product teams, IP Legal, and Comms to position new offerings and introduce them to the world.

Key responsibilities

  • Own all brand architecture strategy across Anthropic and Claude, including how brands, products, models, features, plans, and programs relate to one another

  • Lead decisions on where new products, models, features, and programs sit in the portfolio, including when an offering extends an existing brand and when it needs its own, and when something earns a name versus a plain description

  • Own the company's global naming strategy and all naming work from brief to launch, across products, features, events, and programs

  • Develop names yourself, in volume, from descriptive to coined, and help teams and leaders pressure test them, running research when a decision needs it

  • Build coherent brand architecture, naming, and terminology systems, including conventions, usage rules, and a source of truth, along with tools and training so teams can make routine calls on their own

  • Manage intake and review, and get teams to come to us early, while there's still space to think, connect dots, and simplify

  • Present brand architecture and naming decision docs to senior leaders, and advise them on the hard calls

  • Track the portfolio over time, auditing and simplifying where it has grown complex

  • Partner with IP Legal to evaluate options, plan around clearance risk, and take names through clearance

  • Partner with Product and Brand Marketing to shape how new offerings are positioned, and ensure names work across global markets, including linguistic and cultural checks

  • Lead alignment across Product, IP Legal, Brand Marketing, and Comms on where a new offering sits, what its name means, how to use it, and how to introduce it to the world

  • Use Claude throughout the naming process, from generation to testing to governance

Minimum qualifications

  • Have deep expertise in brand architecture, including deciding where new products and programs sit in a complex and growing portfolio

  • Have built or rebuilt the brand architecture of a multi-brand portfolio, including how a company brand and its product brands work together

  • Can weigh branded house, endorsed, and house of brands models, and make a clear recommendation

  • Are genuinely passionate about brand architecture and naming as a craft, and appreciate how it builds stronger, more coherent brands and audience understanding

  • Know the range of naming strategies, from descriptive to coined, and have personally generated and shipped names across products, features, events, and programs

  • Have created systems and processes that codify brand architecture or names, such as nomenclature frameworks, conventions, databases, and governance, that teams at a company or client adopted

  • Have worked with trademark counsel and can plan around clearance risk

  • Write clear docs that get decisions made, and have scoped and led cross-company work with minimal direction

  • Can teach senior leaders why brand strategies and systems should matter to them, defend a system with conviction, and know when an exception is worth making

  • Are excited about Anthropic's mission to develop AI that is safe and beneficial

Preferred qualifications

  • Led brand architecture or naming in-house, at a specialty branding agency, or at a consultancy

  • Restructured a brand portfolio through rapid growth or major change

  • Experience with AI products, models, or programs, or with technology companies more broadly

  • Led naming across global markets, including linguistic and cultural checks

  • Worked across both consumer and enterprise brands

  • Designed qualitative or quantitative brand architecture or naming research, or managed naming, verbal identity, or research partners

  • A background in linguistics, copywriting, or verbal identity, and experience using AI tools in creative work

  • The ability to learn new, often technical, domains well enough to position, name, and brand them

How we're different

We believe that the highest-impact AI research will be big science. At Anthropic we work as a single cohesive team on just a few large-scale research efforts. And we value impact — advancing our long-term goals of steerable, trustworthy AI — rather than work on smaller and more specific puzzles. We view AI research as an empirical science, which has as much in common with physics and biology as with traditional efforts in computer science. We're an extremely collaborative group, and we host frequent research discussions to ensure that we are pursuing the highest-impact work at any given time. As such, we greatly value communication skills.

The easiest way to understand our research directions is to read our recent research. This research continues many of the directions our team worked on prior to Anthropic, including: GPT-3, Circuit-Based Interpretability, Multimodal Neurons, Scaling Laws, AI & Compute, Concrete Problems in AI Safety, and Learning from Human Preferences.
